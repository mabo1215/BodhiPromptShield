Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, "What Prompt Mediation Stops in LLM Agent Pipelines, and How to Measure It," as a regular research paper in Array. The manuscript reaches Array through Elsevier's Article Transfer Service from Information Sciences (INS-D-26-14978), whose editor declined it at the screening stage without review and without scientific comments.

LLM agents copy user content into retrieval queries, memory, tool arguments, and logs. A common defense mediates the prompt at ingress, replacing sensitive spans with placeholders and restoring exact values only where an authorized tool needs them. This measurement study asks what such a layer stops. We claim no new mechanism; we compare nine mediation methods under one protocol on public benchmarks with open-weight agents.

Main findings:
- The detector sets identifier exposure: exact identifiers in agent actions fall from 13.7% to 2.1-3.1%.
- In a live agent, placement decides the outcome; mediating only the prompt leaves most tool-returned values in context.
- Mediation does not stop prompt injection, and unrestricted restoration can exfiltrate values.
- Blind human labels show inferability falling from 98% to 25-50%, a fall that LLM-judge and word-overlap metrics miss.

The work fits Array's coverage of cyber security and of intelligent systems: it is a reproducible, human-validated evaluation of information leakage in LLM-based systems, with bootstrap intervals, ablations over detector, placement, and restoration, and every table and figure regenerable from the released per-unit records.

Since the Information Sciences submission we have completed the author statements (authorship contributions, funding, and the use of AI tools), stated the research questions explicitly, added a table that defines every metric, clarified the presentation of the results, and added the annotators' instructions to the Supplementary Material. No result has changed.

It is original and not under consideration elsewhere. An earlier version is available as an arXiv preprint (arXiv:2604.05793). All authors approved the manuscript, and none has competing interests.
