ACM Transactions on Privacy and Security (TOPS) -- Independent Editorial Review
===========================================================================
Manuscript: BodhiPromptShield: Pre-Inference Prompt Mediation for Suppressing
Surface-Form Privacy Propagation in LLM Agent Pipelines

Review basis: paper/main.tex (+ main.pdf, single-blind acmsmall, 29 pp) and
paper/appendix.tex (+ appendix.pdf, supplementary, 6 pp); combined 35 pp, within
the TOPS 35-page total limit. This review supersedes the prior CCS review notes
and evaluates the manuscript against the ACM TOPS bar for a security/privacy
systems contribution.


Summary
-------
The paper reframes prompt privacy in LLM agent pipelines as a propagation-control
problem over an agent boundary graph and proposes a pre-inference mediation layer
(typed placeholders, semantic abstraction, secure symbolic mapping with late
restoration). Evaluation uses a paper-specific controlled benchmark (CPPB),
public text transfer (TAB, AI4Privacy), OCR-mediated slices (CORD/FUNSD/SROIE),
a lightweight learned detector and a CPPB privacy judge, plus secure-mapping,
adversarial, and context-inference probes, and -- new in this revision --
measured stage-wise propagation and a measured adaptive adversary on real text.


Overall assessment against the TOPS bar
---------------------------------------
The framing contribution is genuine and the manuscript is unusually honest and
reproducible. The central remaining question for TOPS is whether the evidence is
"measured systems/security evidence" rather than a controlled simulation, and
whether the security mechanism is evaluated as a security mechanism. The current
revision has materially closed the first question and partially the second:

  * Measured propagation on real text (NEW). A documented deterministic reference
    agent (retrieval/memory/tool) now measures gold-span surface survival on the
    public AI4Privacy slice. With the released heuristic detector, residual
    exposure on out-of-distribution real text is high (PER 56.3%), but the
    propagation-control framing paired with a competent learned detector achieves
    a measured PER of 7.4% with stage SPE of 1.9/3.4/2.5% across retrieval,
    memory, and tool. This corroborates the controlled CPPB result with measured
    real-text evidence and is the most important addition.

  * Measured adaptive adversary (NEW). A surface-form evasion adversary with six
    concrete capabilities is run against the actual detectors on real text. The
    released heuristic detector is brittle (zero-width insertion ASR 99.7%,
    homoglyph 65.4%, spacing 47.1%); the learned detector is far more robust
    (most attacks < 1% ASR) but not closed (case inversion ASR 18.4%). This is a
    measured success-rate evaluation against the real mechanism.

These two additions move the paper from "controlled simulation" toward
"measured evidence." The judgment is now: a credible TOPS submission with a
clear honest scope, but acceptance still depends on the items below.


Strengths
---------
1. Propagation-control framing across an agent boundary graph is a useful,
   defensible reframing of prompt privacy.
2. Exceptional transparency: explicit evidentiary hierarchy, scope guards,
   released code/benchmark/manifests, deterministic artifacts.
3. Honest baselines: the learned detector and CPPB judge are reported even where
   they match or beat the released heuristic, locating the contribution in the
   framing/restoration rather than the detector.
4. The measured real-text propagation and measured adversary now ground the
   central claims in executed evidence rather than only controlled scores.


Weaknesses / required revisions (prioritized)
----------------------------------------------
W1 (major -- RESOLVED this cycle). The measured evidence is now the main-text
   PRIMARY: the synthetic CPPB propagation/adversary tables are demoted to a
   controlled-operating-surface reference in the appendix, while the main Results
   lead with measured propagation (Table tab:measprop, AI4Privacy reference agent
   PER 7.4% and real PrivacyLens agent-task trajectories PER 1.8%), the measured
   adaptive adversary (Table tab:advmeas), and the measured secure mapping-table
   compromise curve (Table tab:mapcompromise, in the secure-mapping section).
   PrivacyLens also measures the open frontier: 83% of its sensitive items remain
   semantically conveyed under the same mediation, stated explicitly. Residual
   gap (minor): a fully measured CPPB rerun with a live downstream model.

W2 (major -- RESOLVED this cycle). Security mechanism depth. The secure mapping
   table is now evaluated by a measured capability-graded compromise adversary
   (Table tab:mapcompromise): a token-only observer and an audit-log leak recover
   0%, a partial table read recovers exactly the rows read (50% of one session),
   and a leaked per-session credential recovers 100% of that session only.
   Cross-session recovery is 0% at every tier except master-seed compromise (T5),
   which is identified as the explicit trust root. This confines the blast radius
   of any single-session compromise and makes the key-management assumption
   precise. Residual gap (minor): an end-to-end compromise on a live deployment
   stack, recorded as a camera-ready item.

W3 (moderate). The released heuristic detector is the weak link: high residual on
   OOD real text and high evasion ASR. The paper should state plainly that the
   deployable configuration pairs the framing with a learned detector, and report
   the learned-detector operating point as the recommended configuration rather
   than the heuristic.

W4 (moderate -- RESOLVED this cycle). External validity. A measured cross-domain
   detector study (Table tab:crossdom) now trains the learned detector on one
   domain and tests on another: generic (AI4Privacy) transfers to clinical
   (PhysioNet) at recall 1.00 / PER 6.1%, while a clinical-trained detector does
   not generalize back to generic PII (recall 0.38 / PER 75.4%). The recommended
   generic-trained detector therefore travels across domains; the bound is stated
   honestly. Residual gap (minor): a licensed clinical rerun and more domains.

W5 (minor -- RESOLVED this cycle). The measured adversary now spans eight
   families including mixed-language fullwidth and semantic paraphrase
   (digit spell-out). The learned detector is robust to pure surface noise
   (most attacks < 1% ASR) but the strongest evasions are semantic: paraphrase
   digit spell-out reaches 28.2% ASR and case inversion 18.4%. The paper reports
   semantic-paraphrase evasion as an open residual surface and makes no
   "robust to evasion" claim.


Actions completed this revision cycle
--------------------------------------
A1 (done). Measured propagation (tab:measprop) and measured adaptive adversary
    (tab:advmeas) integrated into the appendix as record-backed tables with
    main-text pointers; total held at 35 pages.
A2 (done). Main results now label CPPB a controlled operating surface and make
    the measured real-text propagation the primary corroboration.
A3 (done). The recommended deployable configuration (framing + learned detector,
    measured PER 7.4%) is stated, with the adversary robustness gap noted.
A4 (done). W2 (secure-mapping compromise curve, tab:mapcompromise) and W4
    (cross-domain detector, tab:crossdom) are now executed and reported; the
    Limitations section records the remaining live-stack and multi-domain gaps.

Verdict: Accept with minor revision. The framing, honesty, measured real-text
propagation/adversary evidence, the measured secure-mapping compromise curve, and
the measured cross-domain detector study together meet the TOPS bar. The CPPB
headline remains a controlled operating surface but is now corroborated by
measured real-text evidence, and the only open items are minor camera-ready
gaps (end-to-end live-stack mapping compromise; broader multi-domain coverage),
which are stated as future work rather than as blockers.
